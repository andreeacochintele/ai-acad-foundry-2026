---
title: Co-borrower and guarantor rules
product: mortgages
audience: retail
effective: 2026-01-15
version: 1
---

A mortgage application may include a co-borrower, a guarantor, or both.

**Co-borrower.** A co-borrower is jointly and severally liable for the full
loan, and their income is combined with the primary applicant's for the
debt-service-to-income calculation described in the eligibility criteria. A
co-borrower need not live at the mortgaged property and need not hold title
to it, but commonly does. Up to two co-borrowers are permitted per
application.

**Guarantor.** A guarantor is liable only if the primary borrower (and any
co-borrower) defaults, and their income is not combined for eligibility
purposes — a guarantor exists to backstop repayment, not to help an
applicant qualify for a larger loan. Guarantors must meet the same credit
history requirement as borrowers.

**Removing a co-borrower or guarantor.** Removing a co-borrower or guarantor
after the loan is disbursed requires the bank to re-run the full eligibility
check on the remaining borrower(s) alone; if they no longer qualify at the
current outstanding balance, the removal request is declined.

**Divorce or separation.** In case of divorce, the bank does not
automatically remove either party from the loan; a formal request following
the removal process above is required, generally alongside a property title
transfer.
