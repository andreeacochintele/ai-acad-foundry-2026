title: New-build mortgages and staged drawdown
product: mortgages
audience: retail
effective: 2026-01-15
version: 1
---

Buying a property still under construction from a developer follows the same
nine-step application process as any Standard Mortgage, with disbursement
(step 9) replaced by a staged drawdown tied to the developer's own
construction contract, rather than a single payment at signing.

**How this differs from self-build.** The eligible property types document
describes self-build: an individual customer building on their own land, with
funds released against the bank's own appraiser's milestone checks. A
new-build purchase is instead buying a unit from a developer under a
pre-existing construction and payment schedule; the bank aligns its
drawdowns to that schedule rather than setting its own milestones.

**Drawdown schedule.** Funds are released in up to 5 tranches, each matching
a payment due to the developer under the sale contract (for example, at
foundation completion, structural completion, and handover). Each tranche
requires the developer's own progress certificate, countersigned by the
bank's appraiser.

**Interest during construction.** The customer pays interest only on the
amount actually drawn down at any point, not on the full approved loan
amount, until the final tranche is released at handover — at which point
the loan converts to a standard amortizing mortgage on its normal schedule.

**Developer default.** If the developer fails to complete construction, Moch
Bank's exposure is limited to the tranches already released; the customer
remains responsible for repaying those tranches regardless of the
developer's failure, and any dispute with the developer is between the
customer and the developer, not the bank.

**Completion deadline.** The full drawdown schedule must complete within 24
months of the first tranche; if construction has not reached handover by
then, the bank reviews the loan and may require the customer to begin
amortizing payments on the amount drawn so far.
