---
title: Buy-to-let mortgage rules for rental properties
product: mortgages
audience: retail
effective: 2026-01-15
version: 1
---

A buy-to-let mortgage finances a property bought specifically to rent out,
under the Standard Mortgage product with the modifications below. It is not
available under the First-Time Buyer Mortgage, regardless of the applicant's
own buying history.

**Down payment.** As stated in the eligibility criteria document, a rental
property requires a minimum 25 percent down payment, higher than the 15
percent for a primary residence.

**Rate premium.** A buy-to-let loan carries a margin 0.4 percentage points
above the rate the same loan-to-value tier would give a primary-residence
applicant, applied to both the fixed and variable periods.

**Rental income in the affordability check.** 70 percent of the property's
projected monthly rental income, as stated in the appraiser's report, may be
added to the applicant's income for the debt-service-to-income calculation.
The remaining 30 percent is treated as a buffer for vacancy and maintenance
and is never counted. Projected rental income cannot exceed 70 percent of
the total qualifying income used for the loan — the applicant must still
demonstrate independent income for the remainder.

**Insurance.** Property and life insurance remain mandatory, as with any
mortgage; payment protection insurance is not offered on buy-to-let loans,
since it is designed to cover an owner-occupier's own income loss.

**Occupancy.** The property may not be used as the applicant's own primary
residence at any point during the loan term without first converting to a
Standard Mortgage at the then-current rate — the reverse of the First-Time
Buyer Mortgage's conversion rule.
