---
title: Mortgage portability (moving your mortgage to a new property)
product: mortgages
audience: retail
effective: 2026-01-15
version: 1
---

Portability lets a customer selling their current property and buying another
carry their existing mortgage's rate and remaining fixed period across to the
new property, instead of paying it off and starting a new application from
scratch.

**How it differs from refinancing.** Refinancing (see that document) changes
which lender holds the loan, or restructures its terms, while keeping the
same property as collateral. Portability keeps the same loan and the same
lender, and changes which property secures it. The two are independent: a
ported mortgage can later be refinanced, and vice versa.

**Conditions.** The new property must pass the same eligibility checks as any
new mortgage (see eligible property types), and must be appraised at
application. If the new property's price is higher than the outstanding
loan balance, the difference can be financed as a top-up tranche at the
current loan-to-value rate; if lower, the loan amount is reduced accordingly
and any resulting overpayment is not refunded but shortens the term.

**What carries over.** The remaining fixed-period length, the fixed rate
itself (if still within the fixed period), and the origination date used for
the early repayment fee schedule all carry over unchanged. The variable-rate
margin also carries over.

**Timing.** The sale of the old property and the purchase of the new one must
complete within 90 days of each other; portability cannot be used to carry a
mortgage forward with no replacement property identified.

**Fee.** A 250 EUR administrative fee applies, separate from any appraisal or
Land Registry cost on the new property.
